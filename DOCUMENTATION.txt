THE FAMILY DOG
Detailed game and project documentation
========================================

This guide describes the current files in the project revision. It covers
how to play, item effects, scoring, achievements, saved data, and the code
layout. When a detail depends on implementation, the JavaScript source is
the final authority.


1. PROJECT SUMMARY
------------------
The Family Dog is a single-player, third-person cartoon physics game made
with HTML, CSS, JavaScript modules, and Three.js. The player explores a
small suburban yard, interacts with a cartoon dog, and uses tools to launch
it through the yard and into traffic. Flights, combos, close calls, and
finishes award coins. Coins unlock equipment and one-use items at the street
stand.

The dog has health, mood, and seasonal needs. It wanders, retrieves the
ball, eats from its bowl, reacts to bugs in spring, becomes thirsty in
summer, and can fight back when angry. The player can also pet, kiss, feed,
grab, throw, and restrain it. Defeated characters recover or respawn so play
can continue.

The game is fictional slapstick. Its title screen contains a disclaimer and
links with guidance for reporting real animal abuse. The game depicts no
real animal and is not an endorsement of animal abuse.


2. STARTING AND BASIC CONTROLS
------------------------------
Open the project preview, press Play, and use the mouse to aim. The game
requests pointer lock so mouse movement can turn the camera. If pointer lock
is released, click the canvas to resume. Opening the shop or achievement
panel pauses direct player control until the panel is closed.

Movement and camera
  W / A / S / D       Move forward, left, backward, and right.
  Shift               Sprint while moving.
  Space               Jump.
  Mouse               Aim and rotate the camera.
  Left click          Use the selected item once.
  Mouse wheel         Cycle through owned hotbar items.
  1 through 9         Select hotbar slots 1 through 9.
  0                   Select hotbar slot 10.

Touchscreen controls
  Left stick          Move.
  Drag open screen    Rotate the camera; tap to use the selected item.
  Use / Jump          Use the selected item / jump.
  Run                 Hold to sprint.
  Grab / Drop         Toggle carrying the dog.
  Throw               Throw the dog while carrying it.
  More                Open controls for ragdoll/get up, reload, dog camera,
                      snow building, season, infinite health, the shop, and
                      achievements.
  Hotbar              Swipe horizontally, then tap an item to select it.

Dog and item actions
  F (hold)            Grab the dog when close; release F to drop it.
  Click while holding Punch the dog you are carrying.
  Q while holding     Throw the dog. Sprint first for a stronger throw.
  E                   Open the shop near the street stand; E closes it too.
  G                   Reload while the pistol is selected.
  R                   Enter voluntary ragdoll; press R again to get up.
  T                   Cycle Default, Spring, Summer, and Winter.
  B (hold)            Build snow blocks while Winter is active.
  C                   Toggle the manual dog-follow camera.
  H                   Open or close the achievements and combos panel.
  I                   Toggle infinite health for both player and dog.

The number keys select hotbar positions, not fixed item types. Buying items
adds them to the end of the owned list; Snowball is present only in Winter.
As the list changes, the item assigned to a number can change. Mouse-wheel
selection also wraps from the last slot to the first.

Camera behavior
  A powerful launch or a car hit can briefly switch to an automatic camera
  that follows the dog. Moving, aiming, selecting an item, or pressing a
  movement/action key leaves this automatic camera. This automatic view is
  temporary and distinct from the manual camera toggled with C.

  The manual dog camera stays active until C is pressed again. Player
  movement remains possible, but tools cannot be used while this camera is
  active. Its on-screen hint shows the C key. Returning to the normal camera
  restores the crosshair.

Health behavior
  Player health regenerates gradually after damage when the player is not
  ragdolled or knocked out. A knockout lasts briefly, then the player
  respawns at the yard spawn point with full health. A voluntary ragdoll does
  not automatically end; use R to get up when able.

  Infinite health is off by default. Turning it on restores both health
  values and prevents later damage from reducing them. Hits can still move
  characters, launch the dog, and trigger effects. The setting is saved in
  this browser. Turning it on while the dog is already defeated resolves any
  unpaid finish and respawns it.


3. GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS
-------------------
The main loop is:
  1. Use a tool or interaction in the yard.
  2. Follow a flight and add mid-air hits, snowballs, traffic impacts, or
     environmental collisions to build a chain.
  3. Let the dog land to settle flight coins and named combo bonuses.
  4. Spend coins at the stand, then try different launch and combo routes.

Dog mood and behavior
  The mood meter ranges from -100 to +100. The dog becomes angry below -25
  mood unless its temporary affection effect is active. Anger can make it
  chase and attack the player, reject or escape a grab, and bark or growl.
  At very low mood it can tackle a nearby upright player. A ragdolled player
  can also be attacked. Anger fades as mood recovers.

  Successful petting, kissing, feeding, and giving a bone or tolerated milk
  can improve mood. Chocolate makes the dog affectionate temporarily, but
  also damages it. A pet or kiss can fail against an angry dog and cause it
  to snap. An angry dog may also resist an attempted grab or break free if
  held too long.

  The dog wanders around the yard when idle, may chase a free ball when calm,
  and walks to food placed in its bowl. It can be distracted by spring flies
  and will jump and snap at them. It occasionally poops while grounded and
  active; the resulting messes remain visible during the session and attract
  spring flies.

  The dog has 100 health and gradually regenerates when not sick or knocked
  out. Its lactose tolerance is chosen randomly when the game session starts
  and is not stored in the save. Giving it milk reveals the result for that
  session. An intolerant dog takes immediate and continuing damage from
  milk; a tolerant dog gets a mood boost. Grapes also cause immediate and
  continuing damage. The dog recovers and respawns after defeat.

Player interaction
  Most physical items require the dog to be close and in front of the
  player. The exact reach varies by action. Kissing is aimed: stand close and
  point the crosshair at the top of the dog's head. Feeding from the Feed
  tool is done at the bowl beside the dog house, not directly to the dog.

  Grabbing is done by pressing and holding F near the dog. While holding it,
  click to punch, press Q to throw, or release F to drop it. A sprinting Q
  throw has greater launch force. If the player is ragdolled or knocked out,
  the dog is dropped.

  The reusable Ball can be thrown and retrieved. A calm dog may fetch it and
  return it to the player. A loose ball can also return automatically when
  the player approaches it after it has been out for a short time.

  Glue is a spray tool. Sustained spray builds glue on the dog. A sufficiently
  sticky dog can stick to a surface after a collision or to the ground after
  a fall; wall attachment is tracked for the Sticky Situation combo.

  The Chain is permanent equipment. Use it near the dog to attach a 5.5 m
  simulated chain; use it again to unchain. A strong pull from a flying dog
  can drag the player or knock them into ragdoll. It is removed when the dog
  is defeated or certain respawn events occur.

Traffic and the yard
  Cars loop continuously around the road outside the yard. They can hit the
  player, dog, or John the Fish. A player hit awards 5 coins and a dog hit
  starts or extends a traffic flight. Close calls award 3 coins. The yard
  contains the dog house and bowl, a trash area, garden and patio props, a
  kiddie pool, a surrounding fence with a gate, and the shop stand across
  the road. Traffic is a hazard and a way to continue or score flights.

  Flight distance is horizontal distance from the first launch point to the
  landing point. A landing is outside the yard when it is outside the
  rectangle bounded by x = -16 to 16 and z = -12 to 14.

Defeat and respawn
  Damage reduces dog health. At zero health, the dog is marked defeated and
  cannot take more damage. A grounded defeated dog stays down for about
  4.5 seconds. If it is still flying, its finish reward is settled when it
  lands. It then respawns at its home by the dog house with restored health
  and reset temporary effects. A finish is paid once only. Landing rewards
  and finish rewards can both be earned from the same flight.

  If the dog flies far outside the yard and is not defeated, it is returned
  home after a short delay once its flight is over. Session objects such as
  poop, snow blocks, snowballs, the free ball, John, and the player's current
  position are not restored from a previous visit.


4. HOTBAR AND SHOP INVENTORY
----------------------------
Starting items
  Kick       Basic close-range launch; sprinting creates a stronger flight.
  Punch      Short-range hit; also punches the dog while it is being held.
  Pet        Improves mood when successful; may provoke an angry dog.
  Kiss       Aim at the top of the dog's head from nearby to improve mood.
  Glue       Spray glue to make the dog stick to surfaces.
  Ball       Reusable throw-and-fetch toy.
  Feed       Put food in the bowl by the dog house.
  Snowball   Available only in Winter; throw at the dog or build with B.

Shop items
  Item                 Cost       Type       Main effect
  Bat                  60 coins   Permanent  Swing; sprint for a Home Run.
  Pistol              180 coins   Permanent  Semi-automatic, eight-round tool.
  Milk                 25 coins   Consumable Randomly helpful or harmful.
  Brass Knuckles       90 coins   Permanent  Uppercut; sprint for extra height.
  Bone                 15 coins   Consumable Raises mood and chewing behavior.
  John the Fish       120 coins   Consumable Summons a temporary fighter.
  Grapes               30 coins   Consumable Causes illness and damage.
  Chocolate            35 coins   Consumable Causes damage and affection.
  Chain                75 coins   Permanent  Links the player and dog together.

Permanent items remain unlocked after purchase. Consumable items are used
up when their effect is applied and can be bought again. The shop and
inventory are saved locally. A purchase is unavailable if the player lacks
coins, and already-owned permanent items cannot be purchased again.

The shop opens with E when the player is within about 3.2 m of the stand and
not ragdolled. It can also be closed with E or the Leave button. The HUD
shows the coin balance and the selected item. Consumable hotbar slots show
their remaining count.

Item behavior details
  Kick:
    At close range, launches the dog forward. Sprint + Kick increases the
    launch distance. A normal kick against a glued dog does not release it;
    a sprint kick can break the attachment.

  Punch:
    A short launch and light damage. If the dog is glued in place, a punch
    only produces a reaction and anger rather than freeing it.

  Brass Knuckles:
    Uppercuts the dog. Holding Sprint increases vertical force. Uppercut
    height can be scored even when the dog lands inside the yard.

  Bat:
    A normal swing launches the dog; Sprint + Bat performs a Home Run with
    more force and a longer flight.

  Pistol:
    Click once per shot. It has eight rounds, a 1.35-second reload, and
    reloads automatically when emptied. G starts a reload early. Each shot
    uses no separate ammo purchase; reloading refills the magazine. The
    camera crosshair determines the ray direction. Shots travel at most
    65 m, hit the dog or the nearest solid collider, and can extend a flight
    chain. The HUD displays rounds and reload status when the pistol is
    selected.

  Milk:
    The dog’s tolerance is a random session value, roughly a 50/50 chance.
  Both outcomes restore up to 70 points on the thirst meter. A tolerant dog
  gains mood; an intolerant dog takes immediate damage, then continuing
  illness damage.

  Bone:
    Raises mood and makes the dog chew for a short time. It can interrupt
    the chewing by grabbing or launching the dog.

  John the Fish:
    Summons John near the dog for a brief fight. John punches the dog; the
    dog chases and attacks John. Either fighter can be knocked out. John
    leaves after about 30 seconds if still active. Only one John can be
    active at once.

  Grapes:
    Causes immediate damage and a period of continuing illness damage.

  Chocolate:
    Causes damage, sets mood high, and makes the dog temporarily affectionate.
    The affection does not cancel chocolate's damage.

  Feed:
    Requires the player to be near the bowl. It fills the bowl and attracts
    the dog. When the dog finishes eating, its mood improves. In Summer, the
    bowl also supplies water and eating restores thirst.


5. SEASONS AND SNOW
-------------------
T cycles through Default, Spring, Summer, and Winter. The selected season
persists in the local save. The world appearance, lighting, plants, ground,
and weather change with the season.

Default
  Normal yard appearance. The dog’s thirst recovers when it is not Summer.

Spring
  Flies gather near the trash, the bowl, and visible messes. The dog may
  wander over to bugs and snap at them. Bug snaps count toward an achievement.

Summer
  The screen has a heat effect and the dog’s Thirst bar is shown. Thirst
  decreases over time. A very thirsty dog can become less content and move
  more slowly. Fill the bowl using Feed; the dog must eat the food for its
  thirst to be restored. Milk also provides some thirst relief.

Winter
  The ground, vegetation, water, and lighting change and snow falls. Snowball
  is added to the hotbar. Click to throw a snowball. Hold B to place snow
  blocks on the ground in front of the player. Snowballs can hit an airborne
  dog to add a flight hit. A flying dog can break blocks on collision.

  Snow blocks use a 0.9 m grid and are 0.5 m tall per level. Each stack can
  be at most four blocks high. At most 90 blocks exist at once; older blocks
  are removed when that total is exceeded. Blocks cannot be placed in the
  road strip or through the player or dog. Leaving Winter clears placed
  blocks and snowballs.


6. FLIGHT SCORING AND COINS
---------------------------
A flight begins with the first launch and ends when the dog settles after
landing. Repeated hits while airborne extend the same flight. The game
tracks the first launch point, last launch kind, hit sequence, hit count,
flight time, maximum height, object impacts, and snow blocks smashed.

Multiplier
  Start with x1.00.
  If the dog was angry at the first launch, multiply by x1.50.
  If the flight has more than one hit, multiply by x1.25.
  Each named combo found on landing adds +0.25 to the combo factor:
    1 combo = x1.25; 2 combos = x1.50; 3 combos = x1.75, and so on.
  These factors multiply. Fixed combo coin bonuses are added separately.

Ordinary flight landing outside the yard
  Base coins = max(1, round(horizontal distance in metres x multiplier)).
  Grade: SSS at 110 m; S at 85 m; A at 65 m; B at 45 m; C at 28 m;
  otherwise D.

Uppercut flight
  When the last launch kind is Uppercut and maximum height exceeds 5 m:
  Base coins = round(maximum height x 1.2 x multiplier).
  Grade: S at 45 m; A at 34 m; B at 24 m; C at 15 m; otherwise D.
  This height-based score can pay when the dog lands inside the yard.

Traffic flight
  A flight whose last launch kind is Traffic uses traffic scoring, including
  a flight where the dog was launched by another hit before a car impact.
  Base coins = max(3, round(horizontal distance x multiplier)), plus 15 if
  the flight sequence contains any non-traffic hit, or plus 5 if it does
  not. This +15 test checks the whole flight sequence; it is separate from
  the named Traffic Assist combo, which requires another sequence entry
  before the traffic hit.
  Grade: S at 60 m; A at 42 m; B at 28 m; C at 16 m; otherwise D.
  Traffic scoring can pay for a landing inside the yard.

Landing inside the yard
  An ordinary flight with no named combo pays no flight coins. If one or
  more named combos are found, it pays a distance-based base (minimum 1)
  plus the fixed combo bonuses. Uppercut and traffic flights follow their
  own rules above and can pay inside the yard.

Additional coin events
  Player hit by a car: +5 coins.
  Player survives a close call with a car: +3 coins.
  Named combos: their fixed bonuses, added to the landing reward.

Best ordinary distance, uppercut height, and traffic distance are saved.
Total coins earned counts coins before spending, and is used for coin
milestone achievements.

Combo notes
  A “hit” includes launch hits while airborne. Punches made while the dog is
  being held are also counted when the dog is thrown, up to eight punches.
  Snowballs add a flight hit only when they hit an airborne dog. Traffic
  entries are counted as their own hit type. For Double Tap, types must match
  exactly; Kick and Sprint Kick are different types.

  “Traffic Assist” means an earlier flight event precedes the first car hit,
  with no Kick, Sprint Kick, Bat, Home Run, Throw, Uppercut, or Pistol among
  those earlier events. “Traffic scoring +15” uses a broader test: any
  non-traffic event anywhere in the flight sequence is enough.

10. LOCAL SAVE DATA
-------------------
The game uses browser localStorage under this key:
  familydog-save

There is no account login, backend, or cloud synchronization. A save is
specific to the browser storage for this project. Clearing the site's local
storage removes it. Values are written as they change.

Persistent fields
  coins             Current spendable coin balance.
  earned            Lifetime coins earned before purchases.
  best              Best ordinary horizontal flight distance, in metres.
  bestH             Best Uppercut height, in metres.
  bestTraffic       Best traffic-flight distance, in metres.
  season            Last selected season.
  inv               Owned permanent items and consumable counts.
  selectedTool      Previously selected hotbar item; falls back to Kick if
                    it is not currently available.
  pistolAmmo        Remaining magazine rounds from 0 through 8.
  infiniteHealth    Whether infinite health is enabled.
  ach               Achievement IDs and their unlock timestamps.
  stats             Counters: pets, kisses, near misses, blocks, poops,
                    bug snaps, player car hits, pistol hits, and finishes.
  combos            Number of landings for each discovered combo ID.
  seenSeasons       Seasons visited for the Four Seasons achievement.
  lastFinishKind    Damage method used for the previous finish.
  bestFinishStyles  Most style bonuses received on a single finish.

Session-only state
  Player/dog positions, current health, dog mood, temporary effects, current
  flight sequence, John's state, the loose ball, poop objects, snowballs,
  placed snow blocks, camera mode, current reload timer, and open panels are
  not saved. Restarting the page resets these. The dog’s lactose tolerance
  is also session-only and is randomized again at startup.

js/audio.js

12. RUNTIME AND TECHNICAL DESIGN
--------------------------------
The project is served directly as static files; it has no build step,
package.json, package installation, or server.js backend. The HTML entry
loads a browser JavaScript module. Three.js 0.165.0 and its RoomEnvironment
example are imported through the import map from unpkg. The Fredoka font is
requested from Google Fonts. Those external resources need network access.
The sound files and project source modules are local relative-path assets.

The renderer uses WebGL, antialiasing, shadows, tone mapping, and an
environment texture generated from RoomEnvironment. The world and character
models are assembled procedurally from Three.js geometry; no external 3D
model files are needed. Ground, grass, snow, wood, and several labels use
procedurally generated canvas textures.

The main module runs a requestAnimationFrame loop. It clamps large frame
deltas, subdivides physics updates into steps no larger than about 1/120 s,
updates traffic and effects, refreshes the HUD, and renders the scene. Short
slow-motion moments scale the simulation update while keeping selected
real-time interface timers separate.

Physics is custom and lightweight rather than a third-party rigid-body
engine. The physics module stores axis-aligned box colliders. The player,
dog, John, ball, and snowballs are advanced by JavaScript and checked
against those boxes. Traffic collision checks use the car and character
bounds directly. Dog flights retain a launch record until landing so scoring
and combo checks can use the full sequence.

Use these locations when changing a system:

  Tool effects                     js/main.js, useTool().
  Shop and hotbar behavior          js/main.js, renderShop/rebuildHotbar.
  Season switching                 js/main.js, setSeason(); visuals in
                                   js/world.js.
  Dog behavior and flight physics  js/dog.js.
  Player movement and health        js/player.js.
  Traffic speed and contact rules   js/cars.js.
  Snowball/block limits             js/snow.js.
  World layout and yard bounds      js/world.js.
  Collision behavior                js/physics.js.
  Named combos and achievements     js/progress.js.
  Finish-style rules and bonuses    js/finishes.js.
  Visual and floating effects       js/fx.js.

  Sound list and playback           js/audio.js plus matching sfx files.
  HUD and panel layout              index.html and style.css.
  Persistent save defaults          js/main.js, Save section.

When adding a new combo, update its definition in js/progress.js. If it
should also unlock a standalone achievement, add or update the corresponding
achievement entry and combo-unlock mapping there. Finish bonuses are a
separate system in js/finishes.js and should not be confused with flight
combo bonuses. New saved fields should have a default or migration-safe
fallback before code reads them, and persistent values should be written
through the existing persist() function.


14. ASSET AND BROWSER NOTES
---------------------------
The game needs a browser with WebGL and ES module support. The preview server
provides the correct local paths and module behavior. Opening index.html as a
file URL may be blocked by browser module/CORS rules; use the project preview
or another local HTTP server instead.

Audio is initialized from the Play button because browsers generally require
an interaction before creating or resuming audio playback. Individual WAV
loading failures are ignored by the audio loader, so gameplay can continue
without a sound if an asset cannot be loaded. Three.js and the font are
external imports; if the network blocks them, the page may render without
those dependencies.

The project has no server-side routes and does not call platform account,
comment, upload, notification, or AI APIs. Persistent progress is local to
the browser storage described above.


15. DISCLAIMER AND REAL-WORLD HELP
----------------------------------
The in-game title screen identifies the violence as fictional cartoon
slapstick and states that no real animals were harmed. It also states the
creator's opposition to real animal abuse and provides reporting guidance.
For suspected real abuse, contact the relevant local animal welfare agency
or police. If an animal is in immediate danger, contact the local emergency
service. The title screen includes region-specific links and phone numbers
for the United States, Canada, England and Wales, and Scotland.


16. MASSACHUSETTS ADDITIONS
---------------------------
The street now has wandering pedestrians and erratic traffic. Drivers swerve,
accelerate toward characters they see on the road, and can spin out on soft
poop. Some drivers call out "I love Choosin' Texas!"; pedestrians say
"I live in Massachusetts!", chat with the player, pet the dog, and may
scold or record a nearby fight. A large Massachusetts welcome sign stands
near the shop.

Dog poop comes in hard and soft piles. Press F near a pile to pick it up,
aim the camera and press Q to throw it, or click to set it down. A hit blinds
the dog or a pedestrian; hard poop also knocks the target back, while soft
poop blinds longer. Put a soft pile in the road to spin out a passing car.
The aim arc also appears while carrying the dog or a pedestrian, or when
the bomb is equipped. The trampoline in your yard bounces airborne
characters, including the player.

The shop sells bombs, land mines, and yogurt. Bombs can be aimed and thrown;
mines are placed on the ground and trigger when someone or a car crosses
them. Yogurt makes the dog remember Obese Sonic and become sad.

The PETA bunker sits by the neighboring house on your side of the street.
Carry the dog inside to one of its seven stations and press E to use it.
The dog cowers and cries inside, and is visibly afraid nearby. Dogs and
pedestrians thrown into house windows break the glass, then jump back out.
A drop from an upstairs window causes fatal fall damage. Windows repair
themselves after a few seconds. Pedestrians can also be grabbed, punched,
thrown, kicked, shot, snowballed, blinded with poop, struck by traffic,
and caught in explosions.


17. E-BIKES, MOTHERS, AND GAMEPLAY TOGGLES
-----------------------------------------
An airborne dog now collides with pedestrians: they get knocked aside and
the impact counts toward the dog's flight combo. Two children ride e-bikes
along the sidewalks and shout frequent rude, profane, deliberately foolish
lines when nearby. Bike riders wobble and complain if the airborne dog
bumps their bicycle. Some adult pedestrians are conspicuously large mothers
who complain about how badly they parent and how much their children annoy
them. Other pedestrians have an expanded set of foolish remarks.

Press V to turn car traffic on or off. Press L for infinite dog love; in
this mode the dog stays affectionate and will never attack the player.
Both settings persist in the browser. On touch devices, the TRAFFIC and
LOVE buttons are in the MORE controls tray.

Riders now tumble off their bikes when you pull them off with F, knock the
bike with a melee action, throw the dog into it, or burst a tire. The rider
recovers and eventually returns. Press F again by an unattended e-bike to
take it; use WASD to ride and F to park. The touch GRAB button performs
the same actions and changes to PARK while riding. New achievements track
knocking riders off, taking both bikes, riding far and into the road, and
dog-to-pedestrian impacts. The Massachusetts sign has a second panel below
its welcome message: "We're all stupid jackasses!"


18. CIVILIAN ACHIEVEMENTS AND COMBOS
------------------------------------
The achievement panel now includes milestones for pedestrian interactions:
conversations, mothers, witnesses who scold or record, thrown poop, window
impacts, traffic, snowballs, explosions, knockouts, throws, and long flights.
The newer bike, trampoline, bunker, yogurt, and slippery-road interactions
also have achievements. Achievements persist in local storage.

Civilian combos are listed separately from dog combos in the Combos panel.
Each pedestrian flight records its hits, height, airtime, surfaces struck,
and the actions in order. Examples include kicking a pedestrian into
traffic, throwing one through a window, landing an airborne snowball,
bouncing one on the trampoline, and dog-then-car impacts. Combos award
coins on landing; first discoveries unlock named achievements. The dog
also has new combos for trampoline bounces, e-bike collisions, and
pedestrian impacts.

19. CIVILIAN-TO-CIVILIAN AND RIDER IMPACTS
-----------------------------------------
Hold F near an adult pedestrian to carry them; aim and press Q to throw
them into another pedestrian or an e-bike rider. Both react to the impact,
and the airborne pedestrian can continue into a second target. Pull a
rider off an e-bike with F, then press F near the fallen rider to pick
them up. Aim and press Q to throw the rider into an adult. You can still
steal the unattended bike when its rider is no longer nearby. Riders
tumble and recover; these collisions award new achievements and civilian
flight combos, including multiple targets, mothers, riders, and traffic.
The touch GRAB and THROW buttons support the same actions.

20. BUNKER ANNEX AND BURGERS
--------------------------------------
The PETA building now has two connected annex rooms with a shredder and
a burger grinder. Bring a carried dog or adult civilian to a station and
press E to use it. Both stations use comic effects and return characters
intact after the scene. The grinder dispenses a hamburger at its output
and adds one to your hotbar. Equip it and click near a civilian, rider,
John, or the dog. Some recognize where it came from; others enjoy it. Use it
alone to eat it yourself. The achievement panel tracks the new rooms, burgers,
and civilian visits.

21. HEAD GRABS AND TOY KNIFE
----------------------------
Aim at a head and press F (or hold Shift while pressing F) to head-grab
a dog, adult, or dismounted e-bike rider. Q throws the held character.
The shop's toy knife uses right click for a comic stab. Hold Shift and
right click to slice. On touch screens, tap USE to stab or hold RUN + USE
to slice. Head grabs and knife moves unlock achievements; head-grab throws
add civilian flight combos. None of the effects show gore.
